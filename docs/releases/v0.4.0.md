Bus parts: displays, sensors, a flash chip and an encoder; the Scope
decodes I2C and SPI; the FPU and DSP instructions.

- New parts on I2C: the SSD1306 OLED (128 x 64), the BMP280 pressure and
  temperature sensor (it can follow the phone's barometer) and the SCD41
  CO2 sensor (Breathe on it raises CO2 for a minute)
- New parts on SPI: the W25Q32/64/128 flash, the MAX7219 LED matrix (8 x 8
  or 4 in 1) and the ST7789 colour display (240 x 240 or 240 x 320). The
  flash has a sheet with its status bits, file load and save, and a hex
  view that edits bytes; its contents stay with the project
- The KY-040 rotary encoder: swipe across the knob to turn it, tap to push
  it; the timer's count shows under it
- Every new part is drawn on the board (lit, busy, or with its reading),
  opens a close-up or sheet with what its controller holds, and comes
  with sample code that runs as pasted; the displays can be pinned over
  the other tabs. The app picks the bus pins, and a second part joins the
  bus already on the board
- Scope: decodes I2C and SPI, from the on-chip peripherals and from
  bit-banged pins. The bus pins are shown once, a row of decoded bytes
  follows each bus, and a line says what the part made of the transfer
  (OLED commands, sensor registers and CRCs, flash commands, matrix
  registers, display pixels). Windows of 10 and 100 µs; the trigger finds
  an on-chip bus's START, STOP and first frame
- Floating card: also over the Scope and Registers; a pinned pot is a pill
  you drag, and a tap opens a wide slider with fine steps
- Emulator: I2C1-3 and SPI1-3, timer encoder mode, the DSP instructions
  and the single-precision FPU with lazy stacking (checked against
  Unicorn). Chip messages for the usual bus mistakes: no pull-ups, an
  address not shifted into bits 7:1, SCL too fast for a part, a wrong SPI
  mode or bit order, a display sent commands before it is ready
- A long press on Reset powers the parts off and on with the chip; the
  flash keeps its contents and the SCD41 its persisted settings
- Faster: up to a third less work per instruction on code with many
  calls, and the screens wait less for a busy chip
- Fixes: SysTick no longer misses its first ticks after it is enabled;
  the LED and timer samples set their pins push-pull (a pin an I2C driver
  had left open-drain stayed dark)

Not yet: registers from the SVD, the access log, the clock tree and the
fault explainer (milestone 4).
